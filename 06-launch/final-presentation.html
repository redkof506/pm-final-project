Slide 5 · Strategy · Module 1, Deliverable 1

The problem hook & value proposition

Bridging StreamLine's strategic crisis to one user's exact moment of misery.

Strategic crisis

StreamLine has no specialty to anchor a stable, growing user base. Without an answer to "is there anything good to watch?", the user base compounds its shrinkage until it crosses a critical mass — leaving no room to fund the innovation needed to win users back without deep, high-stakes cuts.

Moment of misery

Users can't find quality content on-platform, so they defect to competitors, rotate subscriptions toward rivals with prestige titles, rent transactionally, or pirate. This is systemic — a result of ongoing strategy drift, not an isolated feature gap.

Data-backed hypothesis · Module 3

Based on funnel evidence showing a drop from 71% → 29% between Browse Titles and Start Playing, we believe reducing choice friction for "The Overwhelmed Browser" via a Mood-Based Entry Point will drive a +5pp absolute lift in Homepage Visit → Start Playing conversion (baseline 29%, MDE 5pp, 1,354 users/arm, 50/50 split, α = 0.05) — while protecting Power User performance as a non-inferiority guardrail. Go/no-go after 8 weeks or full sample, whichever comes second.

Slide 6 · Research · Module 2

Competitive workaround & journey map

"The Overwhelmed Browser" — a casual-but-invested viewer sitting down for a mood, not a title.

Where users go instead
  • Web search to find an exact title, then locate it in-app
  • YouTube, as a direct alternative
  • A competitor's editorial email
  • A friend's suggestion
"Something light for a Sunday night" — typed almost verbatim, answered with silence or noise.
Core frustration

Natural-language and descriptive search return irrelevant results — only exact-title matching works reliably. That's the pivot point: the moment the tool reveals it was built to look things up, not to understand intent. A subset of users report discovery fatigue leading to quiet disengagement — defaulting to a small known rewatch set, never filing a ticket.

Journey — the four-step spiral
01 · Tries mood search

Assumes intent-based search works. It doesn't.

02 · Falls back to rows

Manually filters 15,000 titles the system's way, not their own.

03 · Choice becomes aversive

More catalog scales up fatigue, not options.

04 · Quietly stops trying

No ticket filed — just disengagement and rewatch defaults.

PRD · finalized must-haves
  • Fixed mood selector, non-blocking placement — never a forced gate
  • One-tap skip to standard browse — protects the guardrail structurally
  • Rail actually filters on mood selection
  • Minimum viable editorial mood-tagging, enough per mood to avoid dead ends
  • Session-length instrumentation attributed to entry path
Demoted · Must → Should/Won't

Segment tagging on every event (Power / Casual / Wanderer) — fully removed since user type and feature-usage initiation can already be defined without it.

The PRD makes explicit what a vague brief would miss: the feature must never encroach on core app functions, and must return to standard search/browse fast and frictionlessly.

Slide 10 · Story

Friction, the aha moment & what's next

Friction

Keeping personas, journey maps, MoSCoW, and effort/value scoring honest with each other. A feature could look like an obvious "Must Have" on MoSCoW while scoring only moderately on effort/value — neither view alone said whether that was a real conflict. The journey map became the tie-breaker: "which specific friction, for which specific persona, does this actually resolve?"

Aha moment

The highest-scored feature on paper wasn't resolving the moment of highest friction in the real journey — it was resolving a step that looked important because it was easy to describe. Prioritization stopped being spreadsheet scoring and became reading a story carefully enough to know which sentence needed rewriting.

Key takeaways

Personas earn their keep only when load-bearing — as the actual arbiter of prioritization disputes.

Journey maps expose friction that scoring models miss entirely.

A framework is a conversation-starter for trade-offs, not a verdict.

Format constraints — like an interactive HTML roadmap — are a design tool, not just output.
