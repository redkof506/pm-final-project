# Problem Hook & Value Proposition — StreamLine Spotlight

> Module 1 · Develop Strategic Product Thinking — ★ Deliverable 1 (Part A)
>
> Define the *why* behind your project: bridge the company's strategic crisis to a specific user's moment of misery.
> This is a living document — draft it now, then finalize the **hypothesis** in Module 3.

## 1. Chosen scenario

**Path:** StreamLine Spotlight (B2C)

I chose StreamLine because the core problem is a strategy and curation gap, not a technology gap — it's a case where product thinking (not more engineering) is what actually moves the business, which is the muscle this module is meant to build.

## 2. The strategic crisis

If we do nothing for 12 months, the company will keep losing market position because StreamLine has no specialty to anchor a stable, growing user base. Without an answer to the simplest question a user asks — "Is there anything good to watch?" — StreamLine sits on the losing end of the category. The risk isn't acute today, but it compounds: the user base will keep shrinking until it crosses a critical mass where the business can no longer sustain ongoing maintenance of the service, let alone fund the innovation needed to win users back without deep, high-stakes cuts.

## 3. The moment of misery

The user is currently forced to look for quality content outside StreamLine. When genuinely good content is missing from the platform, users defect to competitors, rotate subscriptions toward rivals with prestige titles, rent transactionally, or pirate. This is a systemic problem, not an edge case — it stems from ongoing strategy and a lack of decisive action from leadership, not from an isolated feature gap.

## 4. Problem hook

We must solve StreamLine's structural decline in market position by addressing the user's inability to answer "what should I watch tonight?" — StreamLine's quality-seeking users are quietly defecting to curated competitors because a 15,000-title warehouse of mid-tier content can't answer that question, and every month of inaction turns a fixable perception problem into a structural decline.

## 5. Value proposition

For **quality-seeking StreamLine subscribers currently defecting to curated competitors**, we will **turn StreamLine's greatest weakness — an undifferentiated 15,000-title warehouse — into a curated, quality-forward platform (Spotlight)** because **declining engagement, hardening brand perception, and competitor consolidation will soon make this repositioning structurally impossible to execute — it must launch now, while it's still a fixable perception problem rather than a structural one.**

## 7. Finalized hypothesis _(complete in Module 3)_

Based on funnel-analytics evidence showing significant drop-off between Browse Titles and Start Playing (71% → 29% of homepage visitors), I believe that reducing choice friction for "The Overwhelmed Browser" persona via a Mood-Based Entry Point — mood chips visible on the entry screen that filter the rail on selection — will result in easier, faster content selection, as measured by a 5-percentage-point absolute increase in Homepage Visit → Start Playing conversion (baseline 29%, MDE 5pp, 1,354 users/arm, 50/50 split, α=0.05). I will protect Power User segment funnel/engagement performance (guardrail: non-inferiority, no more than a defined relative drop from their own baseline) and make a go/no-go decision after 8 weeks or once the target sample size per arm is reached, whichever comes second.
